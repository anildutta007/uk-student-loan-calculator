# User Guide: UK UniLoan & Parental Contribution Calculator

This user guide walks you through analyzing student loan repayments and evaluating parental tuition contributions in England and Wales.

---

## 🧭 Step-by-Step Guide for Students

### 1. Select Your Status & Plan
* **Currently Studying:** Enter your course duration (3, 4, or 5 years), tuition fee (standard £9,250), and maintenance loan (e.g. £10,227 away from home). The calculator will automatically compound in-study interest from day one.
* **Finished / Graduated:** Enter your remaining loan balance from your online [Student Loans Company (SLC)](https://www.gov.uk/sign-in-to-manage-your-student-loan-balance) account.
* **Choose Plan:**
  * **Plan 5:** Started undergraduate course in England on or after 1 August 2023.
  * **Plan 2:** Started undergraduate degree between September 2012 and July 2023 in England, or starting university in Wales.

### 2. Enter Your Salary & Career Expectations
* Use the **Starting / Expected Annual Salary** input (e.g., £32,000).
* Adjust the **Expected Annual Salary Growth** slider:
  * **2.0% – 2.5%:** Public sector / NHS / steady progression.
  * **3.5%:** Typical UK private sector graduate average.
  * **5.0% – 6.0%+:** Fast-track corporate, tech, or finance career.
* Click any of the **Quick Presets** (Nurse, Teacher, Median Grad, Tech, City Law) for instant scenario testing.

### 3. Review Your Payslip Impact
* Inspect the **PAYE Deduction Breakdown**:
  * Shows exact monthly take-home pay after Income Tax, National Insurance, and Student Loan.
  * Displays your **Marginal Deduction Rate** (e.g. 37% for basic rate earners or 51% for higher rate earners).

### 4. Understand Your Lifetime Repayment Projection
* Look at the **Outcome at Year End**:
  * If your loan is **Written Off**: You will see how many tens of thousands of pounds the UK government will forgive.
  * If your loan is **Paid Off**: You will see the exact year your debt is cleared.

## 👨‍👩‍👦 Step-by-Step Guide for Parents

### The Key Question: Should You Pay University Fees Upfront?
Under traditional commercial debt, paying off debt early always saves money. **UK student loans do not work this way.** Because repayments are based 100% on earnings and cancelled after 30 or 40 years, paying fees upfront can sometimes mean you are simply subsidising HMRC debt that would have vanished for free!

### 1. Configure Your Contribution
* Under the **Parental Strategy Lab** tab:
  * Choose **Contribution Method**: Pay Annual Tuition (£9,250/yr), Custom Annual Amount, or One-off Lump Sum (e.g. £25,000).
  * Review the **Total Parental Capital** card.

### 2. Compare Against Market Investment (3%, 4%, 5%)
* The calculator models what happens if you instead place that exact same money into an investment vehicle (e.g., Stocks & Shares ISA, Cash ISA, or index fund) at:
  * **3% p.a.:** Conservative / Fixed-rate cash ISA / Gilts.
  * **4% p.a.:** Balanced multi-asset fund.
  * **5% p.a.:** Global equity index fund.
  * **Custom Rate:** E.g., 6% or 7% for historical equity index returns.

### 3. Read the 3-Way Comparison Grid
* **Scenario A (No Help):** Shows what the student pays over their career with a full loan.
* **Scenario B (With Parental Help):** Shows whether the student's lifetime repayments actually dropped, and how many years earlier they clear the debt.
* **Scenario C (Market Pot):** Shows how much liquid wealth your money grows into at 3%, 4%, and 5% over 10, 20, 30, and 40 years.

### 4. Consult the Strategic Verdict Box
* **Graduate Tax Risk (Red/Amber):** When the student never clears the full loan before the write-off date. Your contribution does not lower their monthly payments by a single penny. The calculator recommends investing in an ISA instead to build a first-home deposit.
* **High Earner Effective (Green):** When the student will clear the debt early. In this case, paying fees saves substantial compound interest.
