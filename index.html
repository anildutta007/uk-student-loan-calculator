What is Capital Gains Tax (CGT) & The ISA Wrapper?

Demystifying investment taxes with plain-English explanations and step-by-step arithmetic from your actual inputs.

💡

1. Core Concepts: The Tax Shield vs Taxable Account

🛡️
The ISA Wrapper (100% Tax-Free)

Think of an ISA (Individual Savings Account) as a legal "tax-free shield" granted by HMRC.

  • Zero tax forever: All share growth, dividends, and interest inside this wrapper are 100% tax-free.
  • Withdraw anytime: When your child withdraws funds for a house deposit or wedding, they owe HMRC £0 tax.
  • Statutory limits: £20,000/year for adults (18+), or £9,000/year for Junior ISAs (under 18).
🏦
General Investment Account (GIA)

A standard investment account opened in your child's name without an ISA wrapper.

  • Only profit is taxed: Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is only paid on the profit (growth) made, never on the original deposit you put in.
  • £3,000 Annual Exemption: Every UK resident can make up to £3,000 profit per tax year completely tax-free.
  • Surplus is taxed: Any profit above £3,000 in a single tax year triggers Capital Gains Tax.
⚖️
Which Tax Rate Applies? (18% vs 24%)

HMRC links your child's CGT rate directly to their salary in the year the investment is sold:

  • Basic Rate (18% CGT): Applied if their salary + taxable gain stays below the UK higher-rate threshold (£50,270).
  • Higher Rate (24% CGT): Applied to any portion where salary + taxable gain exceeds £50,270.
  • Automatic salary tracking: Our calculator dynamically increases their tax rate as their career salary rises over time!

3. Three Practical Tips for Parents

Tip 1
Always use the ISA wrapper first: If your child is under 18, use a Junior ISA (£9,000/year). Once they turn 18, they can invest up to £20,000/year into an Adult Stocks & Shares ISA. This completely eliminates all CGT paperwork and taxes.
Tip 2
"Bed & ISA" transfer strategy: If you have a large lump sum outside an ISA, you can transfer up to £20,000 each tax year from the General Account into the ISA. By selling small portions each year within the £3,000 annual exemption, you pay £0 CGT.
Tip 3
Weigh against student loan upfront payoff: Paying fees directly to the university locks your money away with SLC forever. An investment account or ISA in your child's name keeps the money flexible, growing, and readily available for an emergency reserve or first-home deposit.

Statutory Plan Comparison: Plan 2 vs Plan 5 Rules

Plan 2 2012 to July 2023 Starters (& Wales)
  • •
    Repayment Threshold: £27,295 / year (£2,274.58 / month, £524.90 / week). Deductions are 9% on gross earnings above this threshold.
  • •
    Statutory Write-Off Term: 30 years after the April following graduation. Any remaining balance is completely cancelled tax-free.
  • •
    Interest Rate: In study: RPI + 3.0%. Post-study: scales from RPI (for earnings ≤ £27,295) up to RPI + 3.0% (for earnings ≥ £49,130).
  • •
    Typical Outcome: Due to interest rates compounding faster than repayments for most earners, approximately 75% of Plan 2 graduates never clear the loan before statutory write-off.
Plan 5 August 2023 Onwards Starters (England)
  • •
    Repayment Threshold: £25,000 / year (£2,083.33 / month, £480.76 / week). Deductions are 9% on gross earnings above this threshold.
  • •
    Statutory Write-Off Term: 40 years after the April following graduation (10 years longer than Plan 2).
  • •
    Interest Rate: Strictly capped at RPI inflation. No real interest is charged above inflation regardless of your salary.
  • •
    Typical Outcome: Because of the 40-year duration and lower threshold, over 50% of Plan 5 graduates are projected to repay their balance in full during their careers.

How The Calculator Works: Formulas & Methodology

1. Monthly Payslip Deduction Formula

Student loan repayments are collected via PAYE (Pay As You Earn) automatically through payroll. Deductions are calculated monthly using:

Monthly Deduction = max(0, Gross Annual Salary - Repayment Threshold) × 9% ÷ 12

Example: On Plan 5 with a £35,000 salary: (£35,000 - £25,000) × 9% = £900/year = £75.00/month deducted from your payslip alongside standard Income Tax (20%) and National Insurance (8%).

2. Career Progression & Debt Amortization Engine

For each career year from graduation until statutory write-off (30 or 40 years), the simulation computes:

Salary(t) = Starting Salary × (1 + Wage Growth)^t
Annual Repayment = max(0, Salary(t) - Threshold) × 9%
Interest Accrued = Outstanding Balance × Statutory Interest Rate
Ending Balance = max(0, Balance + Interest - Repayment)

When year reaches the statutory term limit, any balance remaining is written off 100% tax-free by the UK government.

3. Parental Upfront Contribution vs ISA Opportunity Model

Unlike a mortgage or personal loan, paying student fees upfront does not reduce the monthly payment on your child's payslip. Repayments depend 100% on earnings, not loan balance.

If your child's career trajectory results in the loan writing off at the term limit, every pound paid upfront simply replaces money the UK government would have written off for free.

Alternative Opportunity Value: Compounding ISA Value = Upfront Contribution × (1 + r)^t
Where r is the annual ISA/investment rate (default 3%, 4%, or 5%) over t years until statutory cancellation.

Key Protections & Frequently Asked Questions

Does a student loan affect personal credit scores?

No. UK student loans never appear on consumer credit files (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) and cannot trigger CCJs or defaults.

What happens if a graduate stops working or takes leave?

If earnings drop below the threshold (career break, maternity/paternity leave, illness, postgraduate study), monthly repayments immediately drop to £0 with zero penalty or arrears.

Does a student loan affect mortgage affordability?

The debt balance is not counted as commercial debt, but mortgage underwriters factor the monthly 9% salary deduction into net take-home affordability checks.

What happens if a graduate moves abroad?

SLC calculates country-specific price-level adjusted repayment thresholds. Graduates must complete an Overseas Income Assessment form annually to establish their equivalent monthly repayment.
