1. It is an Income-Contingent "Graduate Tax"

Under UK law, what you repay each month is 100% determined by what you earn, never by how much you owe. If you earn under the statutory threshold (£25,000 on Plan 5, £29,385 on Plan 2, £26,900 on Plan 1), you pay exactly £0, regardless of whether your loan balance is £10,000 or £100,000. Repayments start the April after leaving your course.

2. Statutory 30-Year & 40-Year Cancellation

Any remaining loan balance is completely wiped clean by the UK government after 30 years (Plan 2) or 40 years (Plan 5) from the April following graduation. It does not affect credit ratings, and student loan balances are never inherited by children or family members.

3. Plan Rules & Course Start Dates (GOV.UK)

  • Plan 5 (Student Finance England): Started on/after 1 August 2023. Threshold £25,000/yr (£2,083/mo). Repays 9% over threshold. Cancelled after 40 years. Interest is strictly flat RPI (currently 4.1%).
  • Plan 2 (England 2012–2023 / Wales 2012+): Started 1 Sept 2012 – 31 July 2023 (England) or 1 Sept 2012 onwards (Wales). Threshold £29,385/yr (£2,448/mo). Repays 9% over threshold. Cancelled after 30 years. Variable interest 4.1% to 6.0% (capped).
  • Plan 1 (Pre-2012 & Northern Ireland): Started before 1 Sept 2012 (England/Wales) or Northern Ireland. Threshold £26,900/yr (£2,241/mo). Repays 9% over threshold. Cancelled after 25 years. Interest currently 4.1%.
  • Postgraduate Loan (England & Wales): Master's or Doctoral loans. Threshold £21,000/yr (£1,750/mo). Repays 6% concurrently over threshold. Cancelled after 30 years. Interest currently 6.0%.

4. Why Paying Fees Upfront Can Cost Families Dearly

Because monthly repayments depend entirely on earnings and debt is written off after 30–40 years, an average earner will never pay off their full balance before cancellation. In that scenario, paying £28,000+ upfront in parental contributions does not reduce monthly student payslip deductions by a single penny — it simply saves HMRC money!

Frequently Asked Questions for Parents & Students

Will having a student loan stop my child getting a mortgage?

No. Student debt is not recorded on credit files (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) and does not harm credit scores. Mortgage lenders only assess student loan repayments as a regular monthly deduction from net disposable income, exactly like income tax or pension contributions.

What happens if my child loses their job or takes parental leave?

Student loan repayments immediately stop. If their salary drops below the threshold (£29,385 on Plan 2 or £25,000 on Plan 5), deductions cease automatically through PAYE with no penalties, arrears, or debt collector contact.

When does paying fees upfront actually make financial sense?

Paying tuition fees upfront makes mathematical sense ONLY if the graduate is on track for upper-decile lifetime earnings (e.g., commercial law, quantitative finance, investment banking, high-level software engineering), where they are virtually certain to pay off the entire balance before statutory write-off. In that specific scenario, reducing the principal upfront prevents compounding interest. For typical career trajectories, investing the same capital into an ISA produces far greater family wealth and financial flexibility.
